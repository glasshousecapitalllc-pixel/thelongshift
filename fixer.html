Half of being a man about the house is knowing where the stopcock is. The other half is knowing when to ring someone, and how not to get fleeced when you do.

Know these five before anything goes wrong

  • The stopcock. Usually under the kitchen sink or where the water comes in. Turn it now so you know it moves. A burst pipe does not wait while you look.
  • The fuse board. Which switch is which. Label it with a marker.
  • The gas emergency control valve, by the meter. Smell gas: open windows, turn it off, get out, ring 0800 111 999.
  • Boiler pressure. The gauge should sit around 1 to 1.5 bar cold. Below that, the filling loop tops it up. Above 2.5, bleed a radiator. The manual is online; the engineer charges £90 to read it to you.
  • Where the water meter and the drain covers are. Both matter at the worst possible moment.

Damp and mould, the honest version

  • Black mould in corners and behind furniture is nearly always condensation. Heat, ventilate, dry washing outside or with a window open, and clean it with a proper mould spray. Then the hard part: keep doing it.
  • A tide mark a metre up a ground-floor wall is rising damp or a leak. That is a call, not a spray.
  • Renting: mould is the landlord's problem by law since 2025, with time limits. Report it in writing, with photos, dated. Then again. Shelter and Citizens Advice will back you.

Hiring a tradesman without regret

  • Three quotes, in writing, itemised. A quote is a fixed price. An estimate is a guess he can revise. Ask which one you're holding.
  • Never pay in full up front. A deposit for materials is normal, a third at most. Stage payments on big jobs, final payment when you are happy, not when he is.
  • Gas work: Gas Safe registered, check the card against the register online. Electrics beyond a socket face: a registered electrician who will certify it, or you cannot sell the house without a fight.
  • Ask the last customer, not the review site. A good tradesman gives you a name and a number without blinking.
  • Ghosted after a quote? It means he's busy, and busy is a good sign. The one who can start tomorrow is the one to worry about.
  • Paid by card or over £100 on credit: Section 75 can get it back if the work never happens. Bank transfer to a man with a van cannot.

Jobs worth doing yourself

  • Bleeding radiators, changing a tap washer or a whole tap, re-sealing a bath, fixing a running loo, hanging anything, painting anything, patching plaster, fitting a smoke alarm.
  • Jobs not worth doing yourself: anything with gas, anything in the consumer unit, anything on the roof, anything structural, anything your partner has already asked you about three times.

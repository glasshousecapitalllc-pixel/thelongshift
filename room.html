The Room

Men don't talk face to face. They talk shoulder to shoulder: at the match, over an engine, in a back room on a Monday night with a brew. This is that room, and the plan is to open one in every town that has a man willing to turn up twice.

Nights near you

Want to start one? You need a room, a kettle, a fixed night and the nerve to sit there alone the first week. We'll put it on this page and send men your way.

How a night runs

  • Same place, same night, same time, every week. Men come back to things that don't move.
  • Free. Nobody pays to talk.
  • Chairs in a circle, or a pitch, or a bench. Doing something with your hands or feet makes the talking easier, not harder.
  • Three questions, every week: how was your week, one thing that went right, anything you want to get off your chest. Then whatever happens, happens.
  • What is said in the room stays in the room. Nobody fixes anybody. Nobody sells anything.
  • First names or no names. Nobody is asked anything they don't offer.

Already open, every Monday, all over the country

  • Andy's Man Club: hundreds of groups, Mondays 7pm except bank holidays, free, walk in. Thousands of men a week. It started with nine in Halifax.
  • Talk Club: "how are you, out of ten?" Talking groups, sports groups, free.
  • Men's Sheds: a thousand workshops where the fixing is the point and the talking is the side effect.
  • None of them is about fatherhood. All of them are full of fathers. Go anyway. Our nights are for when you want the other dads in the room too.

Five-a-side, and the rest

  • A kickabout with a WhatsApp group attached is the most reliable men's mental health service in Britain, and it has no funding and no logo.
  • If your town has a stream here, the five-a-side, the parkrun pair and the Saturday morning coffee get organised in it. That is what it is for.
  • Bring your kids when it fits. A dads-and-kids kickabout on a Sunday morning is the easiest first step into a room there is, and nobody has to say anything.

If tonight is bad, the Samaritans answer on 116 123, free, any hour. If you are thinking about ending your life, call 999 or go to A&E. Monday will still be there.

    If you're separated and the numbers have started

    • Keep every message about money in writing. Texts count. Shouting doesn't.
    • A family-based arrangement costs nothing. Going through the service costs the paying parent 20% on top and the receiving parent 4% off. Agree it between you if there is any way on earth to.
    • The service uses last year's HMRC income. If you've lost your job or your income has dropped by 25% or more, tell them. It is not automatic.
    • Child maintenance and seeing your children are separate things in law. Neither is a lever on the other, whatever anyone says at the door.
    • Arrears don't go away. They follow you, with enforcement. If you can't pay, say so early and in writing.

    What you may be owed

    • Run a benefits check at entitledto or Turn2us. Ten minutes. Working dads miss Universal Credit top-ups and council tax reductions every day.
    • Child Benefit: claim it even if you earn too much to keep it, because it protects your partner's state pension record. Over about £60,000 you start paying some back; over about £80,000 all of it.
    • Tax-Free Childcare pays £2 for every £8 you put in, up to £2,000 a child a year. Separate from the free hours.
    • Marriage Allowance if one of you earns under the personal allowance: a few hundred pounds a year, backdated four years.

    If the debt has got away from you

    • StepChange and National Debtline are free and do not judge. The firms that advertise are not free.
    • Priority debts first: rent or mortgage, council tax, energy, court fines, child maintenance. Credit cards shout loudest and matter least.
    • Breathing Space: 60 days with interest and enforcement frozen, through a debt adviser. It exists. Use it.
    • Tell your partner before the letters do. The debt is bad. The secret is worse.
